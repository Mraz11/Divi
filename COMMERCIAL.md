# Using Dīvi: free licence, commercial licence, citation

Dīvi has two licences. You choose the one that fits what you do.

## 1. The free licence (AGPL-3.0-or-later)

Anyone, in any country, may use, study, change and share Dīvi under the
[GNU Affero General Public License](LICENSE). It costs nothing. The condition is the AGPL's own:
if you give Dīvi, or a changed version of it, to others, or let others use it over a network,
you must give them the source code of your version under the same licence.

Research, teaching, government and non-profit work normally fits this licence with no further
step.

## 2. The commercial licence

If you cannot meet the AGPL's condition, you need a commercial licence from the copyright
holder. Typical cases:

- Dīvi built into a product or service whose source code stays closed.
- Dīvi offered to customers as a hosted service without publishing your changes.
- An organisation whose rules do not allow AGPL software.

**Price.**

| Who | Commercial licence |
|---|---|
| Organisations based in Bangladesh | Free of charge |
| Organisations based in a country the World Bank lists as low income in the year of the request | Free of charge |
| Everyone else | Paid, or in exchange for a collaboration agreed in writing (shared field data, co-authored work, or development) |

The World Health Organization uses the World Bank's income groups, so that list is the reference.
A free commercial licence is still a signed licence: ask for it.

**How to ask.** Open an issue titled "Licence request" at
<https://github.com/Mraz11/Divi/issues> and say who you are and what you want to do. Do not
put private details in the issue; you will be contacted.

## 3. Citation

Under either licence, if you publish results made with Dīvi (a paper, a report, a thesis, a
map), cite it. The details are in [CITATION.cff](CITATION.cff); GitHub shows them under
"Cite this repository". Dīvi prints the same line in its project record.

Citation is a request under the free licence, because the AGPL does not allow extra conditions.
It is a condition of the commercial licence.

## Contributions

A commercial licence can only be offered if one party holds the rights to all of the code. So
contributions are accepted only with a signed contributor agreement that gives the project the
right to license them under both licences. See [docs/LICENSING.md](docs/LICENSING.md).

*This page describes the licensing policy. It is not legal advice, and the commercial licence
text itself is agreed case by case.*
